# HR ANALYTICS-Dashboard
### HR ANALYTICS

### Purpose 
The purpose of this HR Analysis Dashboard is to provide a comprehensive and visual overview of the organization’s 
workforce data to help HR professionals and management make informed,
data-driven decisions.


### Tech Stack

The dashboard was built using the following tools and technologies:

• 📊 Power BI Desktop – Main data visualization platform used for report creation.

• 📂 Power Query – Data transformation and cleaning layer for reshaping and preparing the data.

• 🧠 DAX (Data Analysis Expressions) – Used for calculated measures, dynamic visuals, and conditional logic.

• 📝 Data Modeling – Relationships established among tables (resorts, snow, and data_dictionary) to enable cross-filtering and aggregation.

• 📁 File Format – .pbix for development and .png for dashboard previews.

### Data Source

Kaggel

### Features:

### Business Problem
The HR department of a company is facing challenges in understanding workforce demographics, employee distribution, and satisfaction trends. They need a visual, data-driven overview of their employees across various dimensions — such as age, gender, education, department, and job role — to make better decisions about hiring, retention, and workforce planning.

Key business problems include:

Lack of visibility into employee demographics and job distribution.

Difficulty in identifying workforce diversity gaps.

Inability to track satisfaction or performance by department.

No clear understanding of how education and age impact employee count or salary distribution.


### Goal of the Dashboard
The goal of this HR Analysis Dashboard is to:

Provide a comprehensive overview of the organization’s workforce demographics and structure.

Identify patterns and relationships among employee characteristics (age, gender, education, job role).

Support data-driven HR decisions for recruitment, workforce optimization, and employee engagement.

Track employee satisfaction and compensation distribution to improve retention and HR strategies.



### Brief about Each Chart and Graph
1. Count of Department by Age Group

Shows how many employees belong to each age group across departments.

Most employees are aged 26–35, indicating a young workforce.


2. Count of Over18 by Gender

Displays the number of male and female employees over 18.

Highlights gender distribution within the organization.


3. Count of Age Group by Education Field

Shows how different age groups are distributed across education fields.

Life Sciences and Medical fields have the highest employee count.


4. Sum of Employee Count by Gender

A pie chart showing the percentage of male vs. female employees.

Males (around 60%) outnumber females (around 40%).


5. Sum of Employee Count by Age Group

Displays total employee count across age categories.

The 26–35 group has the largest share, followed by 36–45.


6. Count of Department by Job Satisfaction

Shows employee satisfaction levels (scale 1–4) by department.

Helps identify which departments have higher or lower satisfaction.


7. Sum of Education and Count of Department by Gender

Compares education levels and department count for males and females.

Indicates whether gender affects educational representation in departments.


8. Count of Salary Slab by Age Group

Shows how salary distribution varies with age groups.

Salary levels decline in older age groups, indicating fewer high-paying roles.


9. KPI Cards (Employee Count, Age Group Count, Job Role Count)

Quick summary of total workforce data.

1480 employees, 5 age groups, and 9 job roles represented.


### Business Impact & Insights
🔹 Business Impact:

Better workforce planning: HR can see which age groups or departments need more hiring or training.

Improved diversity tracking: Gender and education data help in creating more balanced and inclusive hiring policies.

Enhanced retention strategies: Insights from job satisfaction and salary distribution can guide employee engagement and compensation improvements.

Data-backed HR decisions: Management can use real metrics instead of assumptions for talent management.
